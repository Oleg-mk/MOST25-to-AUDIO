# An FPGA-steered Si514 replaces the CS2100-CP (Такт регенератора MOST25 альтернативи Si5319)

> The report was written on 2026-09-24, before this repository existed, when the
> regenerator's clock was a Cirrus CS2100 multiplying the frame rate. The board already
> follows its recommendation: an Si514 steered by the FPGA (listener_gw/rtl/dpll.v,
> block 6 of the schematic). The notes it cites are in notes/.

**Instead of the Cirrus CS2100-CP, use architecture B: a type-II digital PLL in the FPGA that steers a Skyworks Si514 oscillator as a DCO over a dedicated I2C bus. If Skyworks confirms an active lifecycle for neither the Si514 nor the fallback Si548, the choice becomes architecture A on the Si5324C/D, not on the Si5319.** A single Si514 package gives both 45.1584 and 49.152 MHz. It tunes glitch-free by up to ±1000 ppm around a centre that the FPGA sets to the measured ring frequency, has a 3.3 V CMOS output and draws **21 mA, a ninth of the Si5319/Si5324's 194 mA** ([Si514 DS](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/data-sheets/Si514.pdf); [Si5319 DS](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/data-sheets/Si5319.pdf)). The candidates' own jitter no longer decides anything: modern parts have 0.07–3.2 ps rms against a budget of ~8 ps rms, and only the Cirrus parts drop out. Even the new CS2600/CS2500/CS2501, with 18 ps rms of period jitter, have at least 9 ps rms of high-frequency jitter, and the CS2100-CP is discontinued ([CS2600 DS](https://statics.cirrus.com/pubs/proDatasheet/CS2600_DS1346F1.pdf); [CS2100](https://www.cirrus.com/products/cs2100/)). What decides the choice is tracking ±0.1 %, 3.3 V LVCMOS, power and lifecycle, and these are what rule out most telecom DPLLs. The most important finding lies outside the chip. The FPGA outputs fs_sq on a 99 MHz grid. When the beat of this grid with the ring's frame rate falls inside the loop bandwidth, **8.2–11.1 ns pp (0.40–0.55 UI)** passes through any attenuator, and no loop bandwidth removes it. This is not SP1 jitter but ring phase wander: it eats **33–44 % of the 25 ns tolerance** of an OS8104 master and exceeds **the whole 7.38 ns tolerance** of its revisions B/C. The 1.26 ns grid that the 792 Msps capture already provides brings the worst case down to **1.0–1.4 ns**, and both architectures need it. What remains before board layout is the Si514's status at Skyworks and two bench measurements: the jitter of the TX edges at the FOT input after the FPGA path, and the wander of Tx relative to Rx under a sweep of the ring's frequency.

## SP1 sees the clock's fast jitter; the ring budget counts the slow wander

The SP1 limits sit at the EOC electrical input: **tUJ1 ≤ 0.0045 UI and tDDJ1 ≤ 0.01 UI** at an error probability of 10⁻⁹. UI = 1/(1024·Fs), that is 20.35 ns at 48 kHz and 22.14 ns at 44.1 kHz (MOST PHY 1.1, §2.1, Table 2-1). Table 2-6 enters the last link's jitter into the budget as a peak-to-peak value, and the conformance test converts Gaussian tails to peak values as ±6σ (MOST PHY 1.1, Table 2-6; CT §1.1.7.3). So the UJ limit reads as pp at 10⁻⁹ (≈12σ): **91.6 ps pp ≈ 7.6 ps rms at 48 kHz and 99.6 ps pp ≈ 8.3 ps rms at 44.1 kHz**. The literal reading "σ = 0.0045 UI" would be twelve times looser and would contradict Table 2-6. The limits grow along the link exactly as the EOC, the fibre and the OEC add jitter: from SP1 to SP4, UJ goes 0.0045 → 0.015 → 0.015 → 0.045 UI and DDJ goes 0.01 → 0.035 → 0.035 → 0.15 UI (MOST PHY 1.1, Tables 2-1…2-4).

How the limits are measured decides what exactly they constrain. The conformance test triggers the scope from the TX of the transmitting node, and explains that for a slave node this trigger removes all phase variation of the master-to-slave link from the result (CT §1.1.6, §1.1.6.1). SP1 need not be measured at all if there are no components between the chip's Tx and SP1, because "In practice the signals for Tx and SP1 are identical regarding jitter" (CT §2.1.5). For the repeater, Tx is the FPGA's output pin, so SP1 mainly limits what the path from that pin to the FOT adds. A histogram triggered from the edge that produced the measured edge sees the transmitter's own phase modulation φ only as φ(t) − φ(t − τ), that is, through the filter |2 sin(πfτ)|. With τ = 10 ns, at 24 kHz this is 1.5×10⁻³ (−56 dB). **The audio band is invisible to SP1, while jitter at tens of megahertz, which is what period jitter is made of, passes** ([calculation in the notes](notes/most_jitter_and_tracking.md)). Hence the target: ~8 ps rms remains the right limit for the clock's own high-frequency jitter, and that is exactly where Cirrus loses. The CS2100-CP's 70 ps rms of period jitter means at least 35 ps rms of high-frequency TIE, because σ_TIE,HF ≥ σ_period/2. The newest CS2600, CS2500 and CS2501, with 18 ps rms of period jitter, give ≥9 ps rms (≥108 ps pp), which is over budget regardless of the receiver ([CS2100-CP DS840F3](https://statics.cirrus.com/pubs/proDatasheet/CS2100-CP_F3.pdf); [CS2600 DS1346F1](https://statics.cirrus.com/pubs/proDatasheet/CS2600_DS1346F1.pdf); [calculation](notes/adi_microchip_cirrus_other.md)). Every other candidate in this report has 0.07–3.2 ps rms in its own integration band.

Slow phase movement is counted elsewhere, in the §2.6 system budget. Slave PLLs pass low-frequency variation completely and attenuate high-frequency variation, and the master's input has no PLL. So the ring is stable only when M-Jitter-Tol ≥ Σ tW(n) + Σ tDDJacc(n) + Σ tUJacc(n) + tDDJ(m) + tUJ(m). Here tW is wander below 1 Hz, and the acc terms are accumulated jitter below the slave PLLs' cut-off (MOST PHY 1.1, §2.6, Tables 2-5/2-6). In the OS8104 the master tolerance is **25 ns pp for revisions after C, and 1/(3072·Fs) = 7.38 ns, exactly UI/3, for revisions B and C**. The datasheet gives neither a slave node's loop bandwidth nor the chip's own TX jitter (OS8104, §18.5.1). In this formula the repeater is an extra node, and the phase movement of its Tx relative to the ring splits among the terms by frequency. Below 1 Hz it is wander; below the next PLL's cut-off it is accumulated jitter; above that it is link jitter, which the next receiver must absorb. The conformance test's examples show 20 ns of system wander and 19.2 ns pp of accumulated jitter on a test ring (CT §3.2.1, §3.2.3.1). These are illustrations, not limits, but they show that the budget can be nearly used up before the repeater is even added.

The Fs tolerance of ±0.1 % is IEC958 normal accuracy, which PHY 1.1 introduced in this very revision (MOST PHY 1.1, §2.1). Per Garner's summary of IEC 60958-3, Level II means ±1000 ppm and Level I ±50 ppm ([Garner, IEEE 802.3 ResE, 05/2005](https://grouper.ieee.org/groups/802/3/re_study/public/200505/garner_2_0505.pdf)). An OS8104 master runs from a 256/384/512·Fs crystal with a typical tolerance of ±50 ppm at 25 °C, or locks to S/PDIF or SCK. Slaves lock anywhere in the 37.9–48.1 kHz range (OS8104, §9.1, §9.4, §18.5.1, §20.1). A ring with a crystal-timed master therefore realistically stays within about ±100 ppm (an estimate), and ±1000 ppm appears only when the master follows an external normal-accuracy source. A universal repeater must survive that too, and here the chips differ more than they do on jitter. The documented steering limits are ±100 ppm in the LMK05318B, ±200 ppm in the LMK5B33216 and ±244 ppm in the RC310xxB, RC32112A and 8A34001, while the 8T49N241/242 only assumes a reference within 100 ppm ([LMK05318B](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/lmk05318b.pdf); [LMK5B33216](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/lmk5b33216.pdf); [RC310xxB](https://www.renesas.com/en/document/dst/rc310xxb-datasheet); [8T49N241](https://www.renesas.com/en/document/dst/8t49n241-datasheet)).

## The FPGA's 99 MHz grid lets through up to 11 ns of wander, and the loop bandwidth does not cure it

The repeater's FPGA (Tang Nano 20K, GW2AR-18) captures the ring through IDES8 at 792 Msps from clk_s = 99 MHz. Today it feeds the CS2100-CP a square wave at Fs (fs_sq) formed in the clk_s domain, and the CS2100-CP multiplies it by 1024 ([listener_gw README](../../../listener_gw/README.md)). Each fs_sq edge comes out on the first clk_s edge after the true frame edge. The error is e_k = T·frac(−k·r), where r = f_clk/Fs_ring and T = 10.1 ns, so its span is **10.1 ns pp, that is 0.46–0.50 UI**. Its spectrum consists of lines at |m·f_clk − N·Fs| with amplitude T/(πm). Near a rational ratio p/q the error splits into two parts: a fixed pattern with lines at multiples of Fs/q (high-frequency when q is small) and a slow beat sawtooth with a span of T/q at |q·f_clk − p·Fs| ([calculation in the notes](notes/most_jitter_and_tracking.md)).

The bad offsets lie exactly where an ordinary ring will meet them. At 48 kHz r = 2062.5 exactly (q = 2), and at nominal the error only alternates between 0 and 5.05 ns at 24 kHz. Any offset δ adds a 5.05 ns pp sawtooth at 198 MHz·|δ| (1 ppm gives 198 Hz, 0.1 ppm gives 19.8 Hz), and the full 10.1 ns sawtooth (q = 1) appears at δ ≈ ±242 ppm and ±727 ppm. At 44.1 kHz r = 110000/49, and the first q = 1 point falls at **δ = −45.45 ppm, inside ordinary crystal tolerance**, then at +400, −491, +846 and −936 ppm. Here δ is the ring's offset relative to the repeater's own crystal, including that crystal's error. Frequency planning cannot get around this: over ±100 ppm the fractional part of r sweeps ±0.21, which is wider than the largest gap (1/3) between fractions with q ≤ 3. So a sawtooth of at least T/3 ≈ 3.4 ns always has an offset where it falls in band ([notes](notes/most_jitter_and_tracking.md)).

A type-II loop model (ζ = 1, B the −3 dB bandwidth) shows what an attenuator does with this error. The model was rerun for this report and gave the same numbers as the notes (fsq_sim2.py (a scratch file, not kept); [model description](notes/most_jitter_and_tracking.md)). The values are the loop's output span in ns pp.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10.1 ns (99 MHz) | 48 kHz | 0.001 / 0.026 / 0.148 / 0.794 | **8.17–8.23 (0.40 UI)** | 0.64 / 0.64 / 0.74 / 1.16 |
| 10.1 ns (99 MHz) | 44.1 kHz | 0.002 / 0.026 / 0.217 / 0.942 | **8.17–8.23 (0.37 UI)** | 0.64 / 0.64 / 0.71 / 1.05 |
| 1.26 ns (792 MHz) | 48 kHz | 0.000 / 0.002 / 0.016 / 0.102 | **1.02–1.03 (0.050 UI)** | 0.08 / 0.08 / 0.08 / 0.15 |

The loop bandwidth changes only **how often** a bad beat occurs, not its amplitude. The share of bad offsets is about 2B/Fs: at 48 kHz that is 4×10⁻⁶ at 0.1 Hz, 1.7×10⁻⁴ at 4 Hz, 2.5×10⁻³ at 60 Hz and 2.2×10⁻² at 525 Hz. Each window is ±B/f_clk wide, that is ±0.61 ppm at 60 Hz for the 99 MHz grid. As the beat goes to zero, the output reaches 11.09 ns pp (0.545 UI), including the type-II loop's overshoot. At B = 0.1 Hz the sawtooth becomes a transient each time the relative drift of the two crystals crosses a window: wander-like, but still up to ~10 ns ([notes](notes/most_jitter_and_tracking.md)). In the §2.6 budget this is **8.2–11.1 ns, that is 33–44 % of the 25 ns tolerance and more than the whole 7.38 ns tolerance of a revision B/C master**. Earlier assessments of the candidates sought ~41 dB of attenuation to bring 10 ns down to 92 ps, and so they favoured the lowest bandwidth ([Renesas/TI notes](notes/renesas_ti_jitter_attenuators.md)). That target is right only for the out-of-band residue, which a bandwidth of ≤ 60 Hz already brings down to 0.15–0.22 ns. No bandwidth cures the in-band part.

The fix lies at the source. If the fs_sq edges are placed on a 1.26 ns grid, every amplitude drops eightfold. The 792 MHz rate is the one the existing IDES8 capture already uses, but the output needs a serialiser. The worst case becomes **1.02–1.03 ns pp at a beat of B/2 and about 1.4 ns as the beat goes to zero (0.05–0.07 UI)**, that is 4–6 % of 25 ns and 14–19 % of 7.38 ns ([notes](notes/most_jitter_and_tracking.md)). This is of the order of one link's UJ allowance at SP4 (0.045 UI ≈ 0.92 ns at 48 kHz). A ring edge can already carry up to ~4 ns pp of DDJ+UJ from the preceding link (MOST PHY 1.1, Table 2-4), so the repeater then behaves like an ordinary node. The option without a serialiser is error feedback. The FPGA carries each frame's coarse error, which the 792 Msps capture measures to 1.26 ns, into the choice of the next edge. This is a first-order filter |2 sin(πf/Fs)| (−42 dB at 60 Hz at 48 kHz), and it leaves only the 1.26 ns measurement term, that is, the same worst case. This option has not yet been evaluated in detail. On the 792 MHz grid the q = 1 points lie exactly at 0 ppm at 48 kHz (then every 60.6 ppm) and at +10.23 ppm at 44.1 kHz (then every 55.7 ppm). So the in-band case is unavoidable on some ring: the fix limits its size, not its occurrence.

In architecture B the same arithmetic applies, only in another place. The loop follows what the phase detector tells it, so the detector must compare the ring's frame edge with the DCO's frame count on the same 1.26 ns timestamps. One way is to feed the regenerated TX back into an input with IDES8. A detector with clk_s resolution (10.1 ns), or one working only on FIFO fill in whole UI (≈20 ns), as the DCXO note proposes for tracking, would reproduce the same problem ([DCXO notes](notes/dcxo_architecture_b.md)). FIFO fill is fine for acquisition but not for tracking; this is my conclusion from the same arithmetic, not a measured fact. After the fix, loop bandwidth stops being the deciding property. The Si5319's 60 Hz floor leaves ≤0.03 ns of typical residue; a lower bandwidth only makes the in-band cases rarer, and it has a cost. Below the bandwidth the output follows the ring; above it, its own reference. Skyworks warns explicitly that the Si5324's low bandwidth passes the reference's low-frequency wander to the output, recommends a TCXO, OCXO or thermally isolated crystal, and advises against MEMS oscillators as the reference ([Si5324 DS](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/data-sheets/Si5324.pdf)).

## Architecture B wins on power and range, A on being proven

The best implementation of A is the Si5319/Si5324 family with a third-generation DSPLL. It runs from a single 1.8/2.5/3.3 V rail through an on-chip regulator, has a CMOS output up to 212.5 MHz and accepts inputs from 2 kHz ([Si5319 DS](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/data-sheets/Si5319.pdf); [Si5324 DS](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/data-sheets/Si5324.pdf)). The current Si5319 datasheet is the final Rev 1.0 of 2021, not the Preliminary Rev 0.3. One register set gives exactly 1024 × Fs at both rates: N31 = 1, N2 = 112,640 (11 × 10,240), N1 = 110 (11 × 10). fOSC is then 4.967 and 5.407 GHz, with a margin of ≥2.3 % to the 4.85–5.67 GHz VCO limits even at ±0.1 % ([Si53xx FRM, Tables 26–27](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/reference-manuals/si53xx-reference-manual.pdf); [arithmetic in the notes](notes/skyworks_jitter_attenuators.md)).

The price of A is the ICAL calibration. It starts only with a stable CKIN and a running crystal, and it must be repeated after every divider write and every frequency change. During ICAL the output either deviates by up to ±20 % or is off. An input shift of 500 ppm or more from the calibration point may start a self-calibration on its own ([Si53xx FRM §6.2](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/reference-manuals/si53xx-reference-manual.pdf)). The chip holds a static offset of ±1000 ppm if ICAL already runs at the ring's frequency. The FPGA must keep the regenerator in bypass until LOL drops: 35 ms typical and 1.2 s maximum in the Si5319, about 1 s plus up to 5 s of settling in the Si5324. The input from 3.3 V LVCMOS goes through the FRM Fig. 41 network (33 Ω, 100 Ω, a 150/150 Ω divider, 100 nF), and the divider alone eats ~11 mA / 36 mW. The reference is a 114.285 MHz third-overtone crystal with no load capacitors ([Si53xx FRM §7.1, §7.4](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/reference-manuals/si53xx-reference-manual.pdf)). The current is **194 mA typical regardless of voltage, that is 0.64 W typical and up to 0.80 W**. In disable mode it is still 165 mA, so the supply must be cut by a switch while the ring sleeps ([Si5319 DS](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/data-sheets/Si5319.pdf)). On the other hand, A's lifecycle is the best confirmed of all: catalogue pages that offer it for purchase, distributor stock (per search summaries, not checked on the pages) and no match among Skyworks' 317 EOL/LTB notices ([Skyworks EOL/LTB](https://www.skyworksinc.com/search?doc=Last%20Time%20Buy#documents); [Si5324A](https://www.skyworksinc.com/en/Products/Timing/General-Purpose-Jitter-Attenuators/Si5324A)). The Si5324 datasheet explicitly recommends grades A/B/C/D for new designs ([Si5324 DS](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/data-sheets/Si5324.pdf)).

The best implementation of B is the Si514. It programs to any frequency in 100 kHz–250 MHz, has CMOS/Dual CMOS up to 212.5 MHz at 3.3/2.5/1.8 V, and a step of 0.026 ppb. It tunes glitch-free by up to ±1000 ppm from the centre, with 100 µs of settling per update. Beyond that it needs a new centre with VCO recalibration, and the output is off for ≤10 ms meanwhile ([Si514 DS](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/data-sheets/Si514.pdf)). The current is **21 mA typical and 26 mA maximum**. Jitter is 0.8 ps rms (LVPECL, 12 kHz–20 MHz) and 0.25–0.35 ps of CMOS phase jitter in the 1.875–20 MHz band. Supply ripple of 100 mVpp adds 3.0–3.5 ps, so the Si514 needs its own LDO. Skyworks AN575 explicitly names the Si514 among the DCOs for FPGA-based ADPLLs ([AN575](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/SkyWorks/SL/documents/public/application-notes/AN575.pdf)).

The loop in the gateware has three parts ([DCXO notes](notes/dcxo_architecture_b.md)). The first is a counter-based frequency-locked loop (FLL) against the 99 MHz clock, which gives 10.1 ppm in 1 ms, 1.01 ppm in 10 ms and 0.1 ppm in 0.1 s. The second is a one-time re-centring of the Si514 to 1024 × the measured Fs while the clk_r domain is held in reset. The third is a proportional-integral type-II loop with a bandwidth of a few to a few tens of hertz. The I2C bus is not a limit. SiTime's rule requires the update rate and 1/latency to be at least ten times the loop bandwidth ([SiTime, Analysis of High-stability Controlled Oscillators](https://www.sitime.com/sites/default/files/hiddenresources/Analysis-of-High-stability-SIT.pdf)). A "write → new frequency" latency of 0.1–0.3 ms costs ~0.5° of phase margin at 5 Hz, updates at 100 Hz–1 kHz take 2–15 % of a 400 kHz bus, and the wander from the DCO step (step × update period) is tenths of a picosecond. Skyworks advises keeping such oscillators on a dedicated I2C bus ([Si549 DS](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/data-sheets/si549-datasheet.pdf)). Holdover when the ring disappears is simply a frozen DCO word. If the master moves Fs outside the window in operation, the FPGA goes to bypass and re-centres the oscillator. The FPGA already has an I2C master (cs2100_init), so both architectures use it.

Among the other DCOs only the Si548 comes close: the same mechanism with a ±950 ppm window, 200 fs in CMOS and 15 fs of additive jitter with I2C active. It is the one Skyworks recommends for ADPLLs, but it draws 87 mA ([Si548 DS](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/data-sheets/si548-datasheet.pdf); [Si549 DS](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/data-sheets/si549-datasheet.pdf)). On paper the SiT5358 is the best DCO: ±1200/±1600/±3200 ppm, 5–94 ppt steps, 38 kHz updates, 44 mA, −40…105 °C, and the AEC-Q100 family SiT5186/SiT5386. But its frequency is fixed at the factory, and 49.152/45.1584 = +8.84 %, which is 28 times the largest half-range. So the two frequencies need two oscillators at $35.59–113.16 each ([SiT5358 DS](https://www.sitime.com/datasheet/SiT5358); [Findchips SiT5358](https://www.findchips.com/search/SiT5358)).

## Ranking: Si514 first, Si5324 the fallback, the rest fail at least one requirement

The order below is by fit to the requirements of this particular device, not by the chip's quality in general. The first two places share one condition: Skyworks must confirm an active lifecycle, because the data gathered did not establish it. Without that confirmation, the third place becomes the choice. The fs grid fix from the previous section is mandatory for every place.

| Place | Option | Why this place | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | B: PLL in the FPGA + Si514 | meets every requirement; 21 mA; one chip for both frequencies; ±0.1 % without calibration traps | own loop in the gateware; lifecycle status not confirmed |
| 2 | B: PLL in the FPGA + Si548 | the same circuit; Skyworks itself recommends the Si548 for ADPLLs; 15 fs of additive jitter with I2C active | 87 mA (~0.29 W); ±950 ppm window; status not confirmed |
| 3 | A: Si5324C/D | the only option with confirmed production, a single 3.3 V rail and native 3.3 V CMOS; 4–525 Hz bandwidth, digital hold, two outputs | 0.64 W; 114.285 MHz crystal; the ICAL sequence; at low bandwidth, sensitive to reference wander |
| 4 | A: Si5319 (baseline) | after the fs fix its 60 Hz bandwidth floor is enough; faster lock (35 ms typical) | all of the Si5324's costs, but VCO freeze instead of digital hold, one output, and it cannot go below 60 Hz |
| 5 | A: AD9557 or ZL30253 | EEPROM with pin-selectable profiles; 3.3 V CMOS; the AD9557 gives the only measurement in the gathered data from a kHz reference into 3.3 V CMOS (3.22 ps rms, 10 Hz–30 MHz) | a second 1.8 V rail; 0.55 / 0.67 W; the AD9557's APLL guarantees only ±100 ppm from the calibration point; the ZL30253 pulls in only ±500 ppm |

Sources for place 5: [AD9557 DS](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/AD9557.pdf) and [ZL30252/53 DS](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/en/DeviceDoc/ZL30252,_ZL30253_data_sheet_012318.pdf). The other candidates drop out for one of five reasons. They fail on their own jitter, give no 3.3 V LVCMOS or do not accept 44.1/48 kHz, do not document tracking ±0.1 %, need extra rails and watts, or lose to a neighbouring part with the same properties.

## Three checks before board layout settle the choice

The list holds only what the decision depends on. The loop bandwidth of an OS8104 slave and the master's revision in a particular car do not change the chip choice once the fs grid is fixed, so they are not here.

| What to check | Why it changes the decision | What it takes |
|---|---|---|
| Lifecycle status of the Si514 and Si548 at Skyworks (Active or NRND) | the active-lifecycle requirement; Skyworks' pages render client-side and nobody has submitted the status form, so no Skyworks XO has a confirmed status ([DCXO notes](notes/dcxo_architecture_b.md)) | a query through [Product Lifecycle Status Lookup](https://www.skyworksinc.com/Product-Lifecycle-Status-Lookup) or to Skyworks or a distributor; if neither part is active, we take the Si5324 |
| Jitter of the TX edges at the FOT input (SP1) relative to the oscillator's edge, through the FPGA's clock input, global buffer, output register and IO | nobody has studied this term, and the notes call it the most likely dominant one once the CS2100 is replaced; it decides whether TX must be retimed by an external D flip-flop directly from the oscillator's second CMOS output | a scope of the CT §1.2.1 class: 2 GS/s, 500 MHz, trigger jitter < 25 ps, histogram |
| Wander of Tx relative to Rx while the ring's Fs is swept through the q = 1 points of the 1.26 ns grid (0 ppm and every 60.6 ppm at 48 kHz; +10.23 ppm and every 55.7 ppm at 44.1 kHz) out to ±1000 ppm, with the oscillator heated and cooled | confirms the worst-case 1.0–1.4 ns, sets the loop bandwidth (a trade-off between rarer beats and reference wander) and proves acquisition and re-centring (or ICAL) at the edges of ±0.1 % | a MOST frame source with sub-ppm frequency steps (for example, a virtual source on a second FPGA from its own DCO) and the two-channel method of CT §3.2.3 |
| Only if A is chosen: the BWSEL codes in DSPLLsim for f3 = 44.1/48 kHz at N2 = 112,640, and the exact position of the ICAL bit in register 136 | they set the reachable bandwidth and the init code | DSPLLsim and the full Si5324 register description ([Skyworks notes](notes/skyworks_jitter_attenuators.md)) |

## Conclusion

Replacing the clock chip by itself gives the ring nothing while the FPGA leaves the fs edges on a 10.1 ns grid. Wander of 8–11 ns passes through the Si5319, the Si5324 or any other attenuator alike; what decides is the 1.26 ns resolution that the project already has in its receiver. The old claim "the CS2100 fails SP1" stays true, but for a different reason. SP1 sees its high-frequency jitter, not audio-band wander, so the 92 ps figure is no use for choosing the loop bandwidth: the bandwidth is set by reference wander and the master's tolerance.

Architecture B moves the loop to where the 1.26 ns timestamps already are, and brings the clock's power back to the CS2100 class (~70 mW against 40 mW). Less obviously, B is the only path to automotive qualification. None of the kHz-input attenuators reviewed has AEC-Q100: the qualified Cirrus parts fail on jitter, and the CDCE6214-Q1 does not accept 44.1 kHz. The AEC-Q100 DCTCXOs SiT5186/SiT5386, by contrast, already exist as pre-production samples with the same I2C DCO scheme. Moving to them later would touch the I2C driver and the footprint, not the architecture, though it would cost two oscillators for the two frequencies.
